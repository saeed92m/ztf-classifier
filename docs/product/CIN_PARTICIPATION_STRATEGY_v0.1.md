# CIN Participation and Contributor Experience Strategy v0.1

**Status:** Proposed product strategy; no public campaign is active.  
**Canonical source:** ZTF-CIN-ARCH-001 v1.0.0  
**Tracking:** [Issue #251](https://github.com/saeed92m/ztf-classifier/issues/251)

## 1. Interaction principles

- One clear scientific task at a time.
- Keep tasks short only where scientific context remains sufficient.
- Use clear visual representations and simple language.
- Offer optional confidence and explicit uncertainty/abstention.
- Avoid unnecessary registration barriers.
- Support accessibility and mobile/desktop layouts.
- Explain participation, data use, privacy, attribution and task limitations.

## 2. Task experiences

Potential experiences include Daily Cosmic Challenge, Light Curve Explorer, Real-or-Artifact, Find-the-Anomaly, Compare-Two-Objects, Discovery Missions, contributor dashboard, research progress, community challenges and educational explanations.

These are concepts for staged usability testing. They are not implemented product features and must not be prioritized above scientific task quality.

## 3. Recognition and engagement

Optional recognition names may include Cosmic Observer, Stellar Analyst, Transient Hunter and Anomaly Investigator. They describe participation or task-family experience only. They do not confer scientific credentials or imply expert authority.

Never reward guessing, speed at the expense of accuracy, indiscriminate response volume or leaderboard position as a proxy for scientific quality. Do not expose private participant information. Do not promise authorship or institutional recognition without an explicit policy.

## 4. Contributor quality is task-specific

Contributor estimates must be based on validated controls and separated by task family. Do not create a universal contributor authority score. Preserve uncertainty, abstention, repeated assessments, control performance and disagreement. Response time is descriptive, not a standalone quality metric.

## 5. Metrics

Product activity metrics include active participants, completion/abandonment, repeat participation, task coverage and time. Scientific metrics include agreement with independently validated references, inter-annotator reliability, uncertainty, abstention correctness, expert escalation and downstream utility. Operational metrics include task-generation/ingestion latency, availability, storage, queue, backup/restore and cost per valid annotation.

Never use activity metrics as evidence of scientific effectiveness.

## 6. Accessibility and blinding

Record task UI/presentation versions. Randomize task order. Hide previous responses and model predictions from ordinary participants where practical. Provide enough scientific context to interpret data without leaking prior labels. Allow a task to be marked not interpretable.

## 7. Future expansion

Researcher workspaces, campaigns, educational pathways and public challenges are later phases. First validate LC-01 in a controlled pilot. Do not build gamification or large-scale community infrastructure before demonstrating measurable scientific information value.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

A participation design is acceptable only when its task instructions are understandable, its presentation preserves scientific context, uncertainty and abstention are supported, accessibility is assessed, response semantics are versioned, privacy/consent are transparent, and the design does not reward behavior that harms annotation quality.
